# Vision Statement

## What Is This Game?

A **Living World RPG** where the world itself is the main character. Players don't just complete quests—they discover mysteries, build relationships, create history, leave legacies, uncover knowledge, trigger hidden scenarios, and change the world.

This is not a traditional MMORPG. Combat is important, but it isn't the reason people keep playing. The core experience revolves around **wonder** and **discovery**.

## The Elevator Pitch

Imagine entering a world where nobody knows everything.

Not the players. Not the NPCs. Not even history itself.

You are not the chosen hero destined to save the world. You are simply another traveler who arrives in an ancient land filled with forgotten civilizations, unexplored wilderness, mysterious ruins, hidden cities, and legends that may or may not be true.

The world does not reveal itself automatically. Maps begin empty. Cities must be discovered. Ancient languages remain unreadable until someone learns to decipher them. Hidden passages stay hidden until someone notices the clues.

**Knowledge is one of the most valuable resources in the game.**

Players don't only trade gold or equipment—they trade maps, discoveries, rumors, dungeon strategies, ancient texts, and stories. A simple piece of information can be worth more than a legendary weapon.

## What Makes It Unique?

### 1. The World Quietly Watches
Instead of achievement pop-ups, the game secretly tracks player actions and responds with unexpected narrative events months later. Kill 100 corrupted rabbits? A white rabbit might appear and lead you to an ancient sanctuary. Save travelers repeatedly? An old explorer might seek you out with impossible rewards.

### 2. Knowledge as Loot
Information is the primary currency. Maps to forgotten ruins, translations of ancient languages, boss mechanics, secret entrances—these are more valuable than gold or weapons.

### 3. Roles as Perspectives
Not classes, but lenses through which players experience the world. A Wizard senses magical energy where others see ordinary ruins. A Treasure Hunter notices hidden compartments. A Herbalist discovers rare plants. The same location tells different stories to different players.

### 4. Legacy Scenarios
Handcrafted, rare events triggered by invisible conditions. No quest markers. No guides. Players become detectives, sharing theories: "A rabbit took me somewhere..." "Maybe you have to save rabbit dens too."

### 5. Permanent Consequences
NPCs remember. Villages evolve. Actions ripple through generations. Kill a blacksmith? His daughter takes over, crafts different weapons, and tells stories about her father. The world adapts rather than breaks.

### 6. Legends Are Stories, Not Bosses
Ancient Entities aren't loot piñatas. Meeting one should be unforgettable. Some require combat. Others want conversation. Some vanish after you return a relic. Everyone experiences the same legend differently.

## Core Experience Goals

### What Players Should Feel

- **Wonder**: "I wonder what's beyond that mountain." "I wonder if this rumor is true." "I wonder whether anyone has solved this puzzle."
- **Curiosity**: The desire to explore, investigate, and experiment
- **Surprise**: Unexpected moments where the world responds to past actions
- **Connection**: Feeling part of something larger than yourself
- **Mystery**: Comfort with not knowing; the joy of gradual discovery
- **Agency**: Knowing your choices matter and will echo through time
- **Collaboration**: Working with others to solve mysteries too large for one person

### What Players Should NOT Feel

- Rushed to complete content
- Forced down a single path
- Like they're checking boxes on a quest log
- That they've seen everything the game offers
- That grinding is the primary progression method
- That the world exists solely for their entertainment

## Target Audience

### Primary Audience
- Players who loved the exploration and discovery aspects of games like *Shadow of the Colossus*, *Journey*, *Outer Wilds*, or *Elden Ring* (without the combat focus)
- MMO players tired of theme-park design who crave meaningful consequences
- Fans of emergent storytelling and community-driven mystery solving
- Players who enjoy role-playing and building personal narratives

### Secondary Audience
- Casual explorers who enjoy atmospheric worlds
- Content creators who love sharing discoveries and theories
- Social players who thrive on cooperation and knowledge trading
- Lore enthusiasts who dig deep into world-building

### Not Targeting
- Power gamers focused solely on min-maxing stats
- Players who prefer clear objectives and quest markers
- Those who want instant gratification over gradual discovery
- Competitive PvP-focused players (though limited PvP may exist)

## Success Metrics

This game succeeds if players:

1. **Share stories** about unexpected moments ("Wait, the game remembered that?")
2. **Form communities** around solving mysteries together
3. **Return regularly** to see how the world has changed
4. **Create content** (theories, maps, guides based on discovery)
5. **Feel genuine surprise** at world responses to their actions
6. **Develop attachments** to specific locations, NPCs, and items
7. **Experience FOMO** not from missing events, but from missing discoveries

## The One Sentence Summary

> "A living multiplayer world where discovery, relationships, and the stories created by players matter more than simply becoming stronger."

## Non-Goals

These are explicitly OUT of scope:

- Traditional level-gated content
- Achievement systems with visible progress bars
- Pay-to-win monetization
- Endgame raiding as the primary end activity
- Competitive leaderboards based on power
- Procedurally generated content (handcrafted experiences only)
- Instant teleportation everywhere (travel should feel meaningful)
- Quest markers pointing to every objective

## Design Mantra

Whenever considering a new feature, ask:

> "Does this make the world feel more alive or more mysterious?"

If yes → Explore it further.
If no → Leave it out.

---

*This vision document serves as the constitution for all future design decisions. Any feature that contradicts these principles should be reconsidered.*
