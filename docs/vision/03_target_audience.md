# Target Audience & Emotional Goals

## Target Audience Segments

### Primary: The Explorers (40% of target audience)

**Demographics:**
- Age: 25-45
- Gaming Experience: 5+ years, diverse portfolio
- Play Style: Methodical, curious, social

**Psychographics:**
- Values discovery over completion
- Enjoys uncovering secrets at their own pace
- Prefers mystery to hand-holding
- Likes sharing findings with communities
- Comfortable with ambiguity and open-ended goals

**Games They Love:**
- *Outer Wilds* - For the pure discovery loop
- *Shadow of the Colossus* - For atmospheric wonder
- *Elden Ring* (exploration aspects) - For environmental storytelling
- *Journey* - For emotional, wordless connection
- *No Man's Sky* (post-updates) - For exploration and base-building
- *The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild/Tears of the Kingdom* - For physics-based discovery

**What They Want:**
- A world that respects their intelligence
- Mysteries that can't be solved by following markers
- Communities built around shared discovery
- Moments of genuine surprise
- Stories they can tell friends about

---

### Secondary: The Social Collaborators (30%)

**Demographics:**
- Age: 18-40
- Gaming Experience: Moderate to high
- Play Style: Cooperative, communicative, community-focused

**Psychographics:**
- Thrives on teamwork and information sharing
- Enjoys being part of something larger
- Likes trading knowledge and helping others
- Active in Discord, Reddit, guilds
- Values reputation and social standing

**Games They Love:**
- *Final Fantasy XIV* - For community and role-playing
- *World of Warcraft* (classic era) - For server-wide events
- *Among Us* - For social deduction
- *Deep Rock Galactic* - For cooperative gameplay
- *Sea of Thieves* - For emergent social stories

**What They Want:**
- Reasons to work together beyond combat
- Information asymmetry that creates natural trade
- Community mysteries requiring collaboration
- Social recognition for contributions
- Tools to share discoveries (maps, notes, journals)

---

### Tertiary: The Role-Players & Storytellers (20%)

**Demographics:**
- Age: 20-50
- Gaming Experience: High, often narrative-focused
- Play Style: Immersive, character-driven, creative

**Psychographics:**
- Creates backstories for characters
- Makes decisions based on character identity, not optimization
- Enjoys emergent narratives
- May create content (streams, videos, fan fiction)
- Values meaningful choices and consequences

**Games They Love:**
- *Disco Elysium* - For deep character development
- *Baldur's Gate 3* - For choice-driven narrative
- *Red Dead Redemption 2* - For living world simulation
- *The Witcher 3* - For morally complex storytelling
- Tabletop RPGs (D&D, etc.) - For collaborative storytelling

**What They Want:**
- Choices that matter and echo through time
- NPCs that remember and react authentically
- Multiple paths through content
- Opportunities to create unique personal stories
- A world that feels responsive to their identity

---

### Quaternary: The Lore Enthusiasts (10%)

**Demographics:**
- Age: 25-55
- Gaming Experience: Very high, often analytical
- Play Style: Investigative, thorough, documentation-focused

**Psychographics:**
- Reads every book, inscription, and dialogue option
- Creates wikis and lore databases
- Enjoys piecing together fragmented narratives
- Patient with slow reveals
- Values depth and consistency in world-building

**Games They Love:**
- *Dark Souls* series - For environmental lore
- *Hollow Knight* - For hidden narrative depth
- *Skyrim* (lore aspects) - For extensive world history
- *Myst* - For puzzle-based storytelling
- *Return of the Obra Dinn* - For investigative narrative

**What They Want:**
- Deep, consistent world history
- Fragmented lore that rewards investigation
- Languages and texts to decipher
- Contradictory accounts to reconcile
- Mysteries that may never be fully explained

## Emotional Journey Map

### Phase 1: First Hours (Arrival)

**Target Emotions:**
- **Curiosity**: "What is this place?"
- **Slight Disorientation**: "There are no markers... how do I know where to go?"
- **Wonder**: "That mountain looks impossible. Can I reach it?"
- **Intrigue**: "This NPC remembered my name after one conversation."

**Design Touchpoints:**
- Spawn in Forgotten Valley at varied locations
- Empty map that slowly fills
- First NPC interaction shows memory system
- No tutorial hand-holding; learn by doing
- First small discovery (hidden path, rare plant, secret compartment)

**Success Metric:**
Player expresses surprise within first 2 hours that the world responded to them unexpectedly.

---

### Phase 2: First Week (Discovery)

**Target Emotions:**
- **Excitement**: "I found something nobody else has!"
- **Pride**: "My role let me see what others missed."
- **Connection**: "This village knows who I am now."
- **Mystery**: "What triggered that strange event?"

**Design Touchpoints:**
- First Legacy Scenario trigger (small scale)
- Role-specific discovery that others can't replicate
- NPC relationship progression visible
- First knowledge trade with another player
- Personal journal begins filling with unique entries

**Success Metric:**
Player shares a discovery or theory with another player or online community.

---

### Phase 3: First Month (Investment)

**Target Emotions:**
- **Attachment**: "This is MY sword with MY history."
- **Responsibility**: "If I don't help this village, it will suffer."
- **Belonging**: "People here know my reputation."
- **Anticipation**: "I wonder what will happen when..."

**Design Touchpoints:**
- Weapon/item with personalized history
- Visible world changes from player actions
- NPC references past interactions unprompted
- First major Legacy Scenario resolution
- Community recognizes player's expertise in some area

**Success Metric:**
Player logs in specifically to check on world changes or NPC relationships, not just for rewards.

---

### Phase 4: Long-Term (Legacy)

**Target Emotions:**
- **Accomplishment**: "My name is recorded in that library."
- **Nostalgia**: "Remember when we figured out the Eclipse Gate together?"
- **Stewardship**: "I need to protect what we've built here."
- **Continued Wonder**: "After all this time, I still find new things."

**Design Touchpoints:**
- Statue, record, or monument bearing player's name
- Multi-generational NPC relationships (met parent, now helping child)
- Player-created content (maps, guides) used by newcomers
- Still discovering areas or triggers after hundreds of hours
- Server-wide events shaped by collective player history

**Success Metric:**
Player returns after extended absence and feels the world remembers them and has evolved.

## Emotional Anti-Goals

These are emotions we explicitly want to AVOID:

### ❌ Frustration from Obscurity
- **Wrong**: Players can't progress because they can't find critical path
- **Right**: Multiple ways to discover; community can collaborate

### ❌ FOMO from Missing Content
- **Wrong**: "I missed the event and can never experience it"
- **Right**: "I missed the world-first reward, but can still experience the content"

### ❌ Overwhelm from Complexity
- **Wrong**: Too many systems to track simultaneously
- **Right**: Systems reveal gradually through natural play

### ❌ Pointlessness from Lack of Impact
- **Wrong**: "Nothing I do matters"
- **Right**: "My choices changed something, even if small"

### ❌ Isolation from Solo Focus
- **Wrong**: No reason to interact with other players
- **Right**: Natural cooperation opportunities without forcing grouping

### ❌ Predictability from Pattern Recognition
- **Wrong**: "I know exactly what will happen next"
- **Right**: "I think I know, but I might be surprised"

## Player Retention Strategy

### Hook (First 2 Hours)
- Unique spawn experience
- First "the world noticed me" moment
- Beautiful, mysterious environment
- No overwhelming UI or tutorials

### Habit (First 2 Weeks)
- Daily/weekly world changes to observe
- NPC relationships developing
- Personal journal filling
- Community theories to follow

### Investment (First 2 Months)
- Named items with history
- Reputation in multiple regions
- Unresolved mysteries to solve
- Legacy Scenarios in progress

### Legacy (6+ Months)
- Permanent marks on the world
- Multi-generational stories
- Mentorship of new players
- Continued discovery of rare content

## Accessibility Considerations

### Cognitive Load
- **Challenge**: Complex systems with invisible counters
- **Solution**: Personal journal provides hints without spoilers; community resources allowed

### Time Commitment
- **Challenge**: Fear of missing time-limited events
- **Solution**: Most content available regardless of timing; world-first rewards are cosmetic/historical only

### Social Anxiety
- **Challenge**: Heavy emphasis on community
- **Solution**: Solo play viable; multiplayer optional for most content; asynchronous collaboration supported

### Difficulty Spectrum
- **Challenge**: No level gates means players can encounter tough content early
- **Solution**: Clear visual telegraphing; multiple solutions (combat, stealth, negotiation, avoidance); retreat always possible

## Success Metrics Dashboard

### Quantitative Metrics
- Average session length (target: 45-90 minutes)
- Return rate after 7 days (target: >60%)
- Return rate after 30 days (target: >40%)
- Average number of unique locations discovered per week
- Number of player-created maps shared
- Trading volume of knowledge items vs. material goods
- Legacy Scenario trigger rate (should be rare: <5% of players per month)

### Qualitative Metrics
- Sentiment analysis of community discussions (Discord, Reddit, Steam)
- Frequency of "Wait, the game remembered that?" stories
- Theory-crafting activity levels
- Content creator coverage (YouTube, Twitch, blogs)
- Player-submitted stories and screenshots
- Wiki completeness and activity

### Red Flag Metrics
- High drop-off before first Legacy Scenario resolves
- Complaints about "nothing to do" (indicates exploration failure)
- Complaints about "too grindy" (indicates progression issues)
- Community toxicity around information hoarding
- Players feeling forced into groups against their preference
