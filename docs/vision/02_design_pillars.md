# Core Design Pillars

These nine pillars form the foundation of every design decision. Any feature that doesn't support at least one pillar should be questioned or removed.

---

## 1. Wonder ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Definition:** The feeling of awe, curiosity, and mystery that drives players to explore and experiment.

### Implementation Guidelines
- Every session should make players ask: "What happens if I do this?" / "Has anyone ever seen this?" / "What's behind this mountain?"
- Include unexplainable phenomena that don't need immediate answers
- Design environments that inspire questions rather than provide answers
- Reward curiosity with unexpected discoveries
- Never fully explain everything; leave room for imagination

### Examples
- A mountain where gravity points sideways
- A forest where paths rearrange every dawn
- A staircase descending into the sky
- Rivers flowing uphill
- Floating rocks orbiting a giant crystal

### Success Indicator
Players regularly express genuine surprise and share theories about mysteries they haven't solved.

---

## 2. Living World ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Definition:** The world changes, remembers, and evolves based on player actions and time.

### Implementation Guidelines
- NPCs remember interactions and treat players differently over time
- Villages evolve based on player choices (prosper, decline, transform)
- World events have lasting consequences
- Geography can change permanently (bridges rebuilt, cities unlocked)
- History is written by player actions (statues, records, legends)
- Nothing is static; the world breathes and adapts

### Examples
- Kill a blacksmith → His daughter takes over with different inventory and personality
- Save a village → They build a monument with your name
- Ignore bandits → Trade routes collapse, prices rise
- Complete a major quest → New regions become accessible

### Success Indicator
Players return after absence and notice tangible changes that resulted from their (or others') actions.

---

## 3. Knowledge ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Definition:** Information is the primary currency and most valuable resource.

### Implementation Guidelines
- Maps begin empty and must be discovered/filled
- Ancient languages require learning (dictionary fragments, grammar books, translators)
- Hidden passages stay hidden until clues are noticed
- Boss mechanics must be learned through observation/trial, not tooltips
- Players trade information (maps, strategies, secrets) as valuable commodities
- Books and texts unlock puzzles when combined
- Rumors may be true or false; verification is part of gameplay

### Examples
- A map to a forgotten ruin worth more than a legendary sword
- Translating an ancient text reveals a hidden dungeon entrance
- Learning a monster's weakness through observation, not a wiki
- Discovering that combining three books reveals a secret kingdom

### Success Indicator
Players actively discuss, trade, and debate information. Knowledge gaps create natural cooperation.

---

## 4. Legacy Scenarios ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Definition:** Hidden, handcrafted events triggered by invisible counters and specific conditions, creating unique personal stories.

### Implementation Guidelines
- No visible progress bars or achievement notifications
- Triggers are complex and multi-factorial (actions + items + timing + location + history)
- Rewards are narrative-focused (unique access, relationships, titles, knowledge) rather than just power
- Different players experience different scenarios based on their playstyle
- Community must collaborate to discover triggers through experimentation
- Scenarios are rare and memorable, not common occurrences

### Examples
- Kill 70-120 corrupted rabbits + save 3 dens + spring season → White rabbit leads to sanctuary
- Help travelers 50+ times → Old explorer seeks you out with impossible reward
- Spare enemies repeatedly → One returns months later as an ally
- Plant 100 trees → Druids grant access to hidden grove

### Success Indicator
Players share stories like "Wait, the game remembered that?" and communities form around decoding triggers.

---

## 5. Roles ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆

**Definition:** Character roles are perspectives that shape how players experience the world, not rigid classes.

### Implementation Guidelines
- Each role notices different things in the same environment
- Roles enable unique interactions but don't lock content permanently
- Players can develop secondary skills outside their role
- Role identity influences NPC reactions and scenario availability
- Information asymmetry encourages cooperation (different roles see different clues)

### Examples
- **Wizard**: Senses magical energy in ordinary ruins
- **Treasure Hunter**: Notices hidden compartments in temples
- **Herbalist**: Identifies rare plants on dangerous cliffs
- **Nightborn**: Stronger at night, weaker during day, better night vision
- **Blacksmith**: Sees ore deposits others miss, crafts/repairs better

### Success Indicator
Two players visiting the same location have genuinely different experiences and discoveries to share.

---

## 6. Exploration ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Definition:** True discovery of unexplainable geography, hidden mysteries, and the unknown.

### Implementation Guidelines
- No quest markers pointing to every objective
- Travel feels meaningful (no instant teleportation everywhere)
- Hidden villages, moving forests, floating islands exist off the beaten path
- Geography follows mysterious rules, not game logic
- Some areas require specific conditions (celestial events, artifacts, group composition)
- Exploration itself is rewarding, not just the destination

### Examples
- A dungeon that only opens during an eclipse
- An underwater ruin requiring water breathing (not just swimming)
- A temple requiring five players to activate distant switches simultaneously
- A bridge that appears only in fog

### Success Indicator
Players spend sessions simply wandering and discovering, not rushing to objectives.

---

## 7. Community ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Definition:** Collective detective work where players share theories, discoveries, and knowledge.

### Implementation Guidelines
- Mysteries too large for one player encourage collaboration
- Information asymmetry creates natural trading opportunities
- World-first achievements are celebrated publicly (statues, records, titles)
- Players can create and share maps with notes
- Rumors spread through player networks, not just NPCs
- Community knowledge becomes part of gameplay meta

### Examples
- "Has anyone figured out how to open the Eclipse Gate?"
- "I heard the Treasure Hunter role can find something special in the Northern Ruins"
- Player-created map marketplaces with rated accuracy
- Discord/Reddit filled with theories, not just guides

### Success Indicator
External communities (Discord, Reddit, YouTube) buzz with theories, collaborations, and shared discoveries.

---

## 8. Personal Story ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆

**Definition:** Each player's journey is unique, shaped by individual choices, relationships, and discoveries.

### Implementation Guidelines
- Multiple narrative paths through content
- Choices have long-term consequences that diverge experiences
- Personal journals track individual discoveries (different for every player)
- Relationships with NPCs develop uniquely per player
- No two players have identical memories of the game
- Individual achievements recorded in world history

### Examples
- Player A befriends a dragon; Player B slays it; Player C helps it migrate
- Your journal contains sketches of creatures you've seen; mine contains different ones
- An NPC gives you a key because you helped their family; another player never meets them
- Your character is known as "The Protector" in one region, "The Stranger" in another

### Success Indicator
Players compare notes and realize they had fundamentally different experiences in the same world.

---

## 9. Consequences ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Definition:** Actions ripple through time; nothing is disposable; important choices echo across generations.

### Implementation Guidelines
- Major actions have delayed, non-obvious consequences
- NPCs remember and react to past interactions
- Objects carry history (weapons remember battles, books note previous owners)
- Generational storytelling (NPC children inherit parent's memories/attitudes)
- No binary good/evil system; nuanced reactions from different factions
- Permanent death for some NPCs with world adaptation (successors take over)

### Examples
- Rob a merchant → Three weeks later caravans stop coming, prices rise, tavern closes
- Spare an enemy → Months later they save your life or betray you
- Kill a corrupt noble → Rebels cheer, kingdom brands you criminal, family seeks revenge
- Weapon description updates: "Rediscovered by [YourName]. Current Kills: 842."

### Success Indicator
Players hesitate before major actions, knowing choices will matter long after the moment passes.

---

## Pillar Interconnections

These pillars don't exist in isolation. They reinforce each other:

- **Wonder + Exploration** = Unexplainable geography that begs investigation
- **Knowledge + Community** = Players collaborating to decode ancient texts
- **Legacy Scenarios + Consequences** = Hidden triggers based on long-term behavior patterns
- **Roles + Personal Story** = Different roles experiencing unique narrative branches
- **Living World + Consequences** = A world that visibly evolves from player actions

## Priority Ranking

While all pillars are important, some take precedence in design conflicts:

1. **Wonder** - If we lose this, we lose the game's soul
2. **Living World** - The core differentiator from other RPGs
3. **Knowledge** - The most unique mechanical innovation
4. **Legacy Scenarios** - The engine for emergent storytelling
5. **Consequences** - What makes choices matter
6. **Exploration** - The primary gameplay activity
7. **Community** - The social multiplier
8. **Personal Story** - Individual meaning-making
9. **Roles** - The lens for varied experiences

## Anti-Patterns (What Violates the Pillars)

❌ Visible achievement progress bars (violates Legacy Scenarios)
❌ Quest markers on every objective (violates Wonder & Exploration)
❌ Instant teleportation everywhere (violates Exploration)
❌ Binary reputation systems (violates Consequences)
❌ Theme-park quest hubs (violates Living World)
❌ Power-only rewards (violates Knowledge & Legacy Scenarios)
❌ Static NPCs with no memory (violates Living World & Consequences)
❌ Procedurally generated content (violates Wonder - handcrafted mysteries only)
❌ Pay-to-win monetization (violates all pillars)
❌ Leaderboards based on power (violates Personal Story & Community)

---

*Use these pillars as a litmus test for every feature proposal. If a feature doesn't strengthen at least one pillar, reconsider its inclusion.*
