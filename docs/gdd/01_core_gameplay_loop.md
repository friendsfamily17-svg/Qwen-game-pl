# 01. Core Gameplay Loop

## Overview

The core gameplay loop of Living World RPG is not combat-focused like traditional RPGs. Instead, it revolves around **discovery, knowledge acquisition, and world interaction**.

---

## Moment-to-Moment Gameplay Examples

### Example 1: The Curious Explorer

**Situation:** Player approaches a strange ruin

1. **EXPLORE**: Walk toward floating stone structure
2. **OBSERVE**: (As Wizard) Notice magical residue on stones; (as Treasure Hunter) See wear patterns suggesting hidden entrance
3. **INTERPRET**: "This isn't just decorative; something activates here"
4. **INTERACT**: Test different approaches (touch symbols, wait for time event, use item)
5. **DISCOVER**: Structure opens only during rain; inside find ancient text fragment
6. **SHARE**: Update personal journal; later trade map coordinates with Herbalist who needs the plant growing inside

**Time:** 20 minutes
**Reward:** Knowledge fragment + unique location access

---

### Example 2: The Relationship Builder

**Situation:** Player visits village regularly

1. **BUILD RELATION**: Talk to blacksmith daily, bring him rare ore
2. **TAKE ACTION**: Defend village from raiders when they attack
3. **WORLD REMEMBERS**: Blacksmith mentions your help to daughter; village discusses building monument
4. **LEGACY FORMS**: Earn "Mark of the Protector"; blacksmith's daughter gives you family heirloom blueprint
5. **NEW ACCESS/PATH**: Unlock access to village's secret underground storage; gain discount at all shops

**Time:** 2 weeks of regular play
**Reward:** Unique crafting recipe + regional reputation + hidden area access

---

### Example 3: The Legacy Scenario

**Situation:** Player has been sparing wildlife for months

1. **INVISIBLE TRIGGER**: Spared 87 animals + helped 3 travelers + full moon + carrying silver amulet
2. **UNEXPECTED EVENT**: Deer approaches (normally flees), waits, then leads player through forest
3. **DISCOVERY**: Arrive at hidden druid sanctuary unseen by any player
4. **INTERACTION**: Druids offer choice: join their order / take sacred seed / learn ancient language
5. **CONSEQUENCE**: Choice determines future scenarios, NPC reactions, available quests
6. **COMMUNITY IMPACT**: Player shares vague story online; others begin experimenting to replicate

**Time:** Triggered over 3 months, resolved in 30 minutes
**Reward:** Unique path unavailable through normal means

## Key Differences from Traditional RPG Loops

| Traditional MMORPG | Living World RPG |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Accept quest → Kill X → Return → Reward | Explore → Notice → Interpret → Discover |
| Clear objectives with markers | Self-directed curiosity |
| Visible progress bars | Hidden counters, delayed reveals |
| Combat as primary activity | Observation and interaction as primary |
| Gear-focused rewards | Knowledge and access-focused rewards |
| Static world reset | Persistent world changes |
| Solo or grouped play | Asynchronous collaboration natural |
| Endgame = raids/dungeons | Endgame = deeper mysteries + legacy building |

## Flow State Design

The game aims to induce flow state through:

### Clear Goals (Self-Directed)
- Players set their own objectives based on curiosity
- No forced quest chains
- Multiple valid paths through content

### Immediate Feedback (Varied Timing)
- Some actions have instant feedback (NPC dialogue changes)
- Some have delayed feedback (Legacy Scenarios trigger weeks later)
- Journal tracks subtle changes player might miss

### Challenge-Skill Balance
- No level gates; players self-select appropriate challenges
- Multiple solutions allow players to use their strengths
- Retreat always possible; no forced failure states

## Session Types

Players may engage in different session types:

### The Quick Check-In (15-30 minutes)
- Visit specific NPCs to maintain relationships
- Check on ongoing projects (crafting, building)
- Gather daily information/rumors
- Tend to home/garden/shop

### The Exploration Session (45-90 minutes)
- Venture into unknown territory
- Map new areas
- Investigate rumors or theories
- Test hypotheses about world mechanics

### The Social Session (1-3 hours)
- Meet with other players to trade knowledge
- Collaborative puzzle-solving
- Group expeditions to dangerous areas
- Community events or festivals

### The Deep Dive (3+ hours)
- Multi-stage Legacy Scenario resolution
- Complex dungeon requiring coordination
- Long-distance expedition to remote regions
- Major world event participation

## Engagement Hooks

Each session should include at least one:

### Mystery Hook
- "I wonder what's in that cave I saw from the ridge"
- "This book mentioned a door, but I haven't found it yet"
- "Three players reported seeing lights in the swamp; I should investigate"

### Relationship Hook
- "Haven't visited the herbalist in a week; wonder how she's doing"
- "The blacksmith said he'd have something special ready today"
- "I promised to help rebuild the bridge this week"

### Progression Hook
- "My weapon is close to unlocking its next ability"
- "I'm getting closer to understanding this ancient language"
- "The village is almost ready to trust me with their secrets"

### Community Hook
- "Someone posted a theory about the Eclipse Gate; I want to test it"
- "There's a group expedition to the Northern Ruins tonight"
- "A player I've never met left a note on my map"

## Retention Mechanics (Ethical Design)

### ✅ Positive Reinforcement
- Daily login bonuses are narrative-based (NPCs reference your absence, world has changed)
- No punishment for missing days; world continues naturally
- Returning players discover what happened while away

### ❌ Avoided Manipulation
- No energy systems limiting play time
- No FOMO-driven limited-time events (most content always available)
- No loot boxes or gambling mechanics
- No artificial grind designed purely for retention

### ✅ Intrinsic Motivation
- Players return because they're genuinely curious
- Relationships feel authentic, not transactional
- World changes create organic reasons to check in

---

## Metrics for Loop Health

### Healthy Indicators
- Average session length: 45-90 minutes
- Sessions per week: 4-7 (varied, not mandatory)
- Players report "lost track of time" experiences
- Organic community activity between sessions (theory discussion, map sharing)

### Warning Signs
- Sessions feel like "checking boxes"
- Players report burnout or fatigue
- Activity drops sharply after initial novelty
- Community focuses on optimization over discovery

---

*The core loop must always serve the pillars of Wonder, Discovery, and Living World. If any iteration makes the game feel more like work than exploration, it must be reworked.*
