# Knowledge Economy System

## 1. Overview & Philosophy

### Core Principle
**"Knowledge is the most valuable currency in the world."**

Unlike traditional MMORPGs where gold and gear dominate the economy, this game treats **information** as a primary resource that can be more valuable than legendary equipment. A map to an undiscovered ruin, the weakness of an Ancient Entity, or the translation of an ancient language can change the course of a player's journey forever.

### Design Goals
- Make discovery feel rewarding beyond material gain
- Create natural incentives for player cooperation and trade
- Ensure information has variable value based on exclusivity and utility
- Support emergent gameplay through knowledge gaps between players
- Enable players to become famous for what they **know**, not just what they **own**

---

## 2. Types of Knowledge

Knowledge exists in multiple forms, each with different properties:

### 2.1 Discoverable Knowledge
Information that exists in the world waiting to be found:
- **Maps**: Unexplored regions, hidden paths, secret entrances
- **Locations**: Coordinates of ruins, rare resource nodes, safe havens
- **Mechanics**: Boss patterns, puzzle solutions, environmental interactions
- **Weaknesses**: Elemental affinities, resistances, behavioral triggers of creatures
- **Histories**: Lost civilizations, forgotten events, true origins of legends

### 2.2 Crafted Knowledge
Information created or synthesized by players:
- **Translations**: Deciphered ancient languages and texts
- **Theories**: Hypotheses about world mechanics (verified or unverified)
- **Strategies**: Combat tactics, optimization methods, efficient routes
- **Recipes**: Discovered crafting combinations, fusion results
- **Analysis**: Creature behavior studies, ecosystem patterns, weather correlations

### 2.3 Experiential Knowledge
Information gained only through direct experience:
- **Personal Encounters**: Unique interactions with NPCs or Ancient Entities
- **Skill Techniques**: Advanced combat moves discovered through practice
- **Hidden Triggers**: Conditions for Legacy Scenarios (often incomplete)
- **Sensory Details**: What specific roles perceive in certain locations

### 2.4 Rumors & Misinformation
Unverified or intentionally false information:
- **Hearsay**: NPC gossip, traveler tales, bard songs
- **Deliberate Lies**: Misleading maps, false strategies planted by rivals
- **Partial Truths**: Incomplete information that requires verification
- **Contradictions**: Conflicting accounts from different sources

---

## 3. Knowledge Acquisition Methods

Players obtain knowledge through diverse channels:

### 3.1 Direct Exploration
- Visiting new locations
- Observing environmental clues
- Finding books, scrolls, inscriptions
- Examining objects with role-specific perception
- Surviving encounters and learning patterns

### 3.2 Social Interaction
- Conversations with NPCs (varying by reputation and relationship)
- Trading with other players
- Overheard conversations in taverns and gathering spots
- Interrogating captured enemies or rescued NPCs
- Attending lectures, storytellers, or scholarly gatherings

### 3.3 Research & Study
- Reading and comparing multiple texts
- Translating ancient languages (requires skill progression)
- Conducting experiments (e.g., testing creature reactions)
- Mapping and charting territories
- Analyzing collected specimens or artifacts

### 3.4 Role-Specific Perception
Different roles automatically notice different information:
- **Wizard**: Senses magical residues, enchantments, ley lines
- **Treasure Hunter**: Spots hidden compartments, valuable materials, traps
- **Herbalist**: Identifies plant properties, medicinal uses, growth conditions
- **Hunter**: Reads tracks, predicts animal movements, identifies weaknesses
- **Scholar**: Recognizes historical context, linguistic patterns, cultural significance

### 3.5 Legacy Scenario Rewards
Unique knowledge granted as rewards for hidden achievements:
- Secret histories revealed by Ancient Entities
- Forbidden techniques taught by reclusive masters
- Access codes to hidden regions
- True names of powerful beings

## 4. Knowledge Storage & Management

### 4.1 Personal Journal
Every player maintains an automatic journal that records:
- **Confirmed Facts**: Verified information marked as reliable
- **Rumors**: Unverified claims with source attribution
- **Observations**: Raw data without conclusions
- **Sketches**: Visual records of creatures, plants, symbols, maps
- **Personal Notes**: Player-added annotations and theories

**Journal Features:**
- Searchable and categorizable
- Shows confidence levels for each entry
- Tracks when/where information was acquired
- Allows marking information as "shared" or "private"
- Cannot be lost on death (permanent character memory)

### 4.2 Physical Knowledge Items
Tangible objects containing information:
- **Books & Scrolls**: Can be traded, sold, stolen, or destroyed
- **Maps**: Physical copies that can be annotated and sold
- **Tablets & Inscriptions**: Immovable knowledge sources in the world
- **Encoded Messages**: Require specific keys or skills to decipher
- **Memory Crystals**: Magical storage devices (rare, endgame)

### 4.3 Shared Knowledge Repositories
Community-accessible information sources:
- **Libraries**: Player-contributed collections in cities
- **Notice Boards**: Posted rumors, requests, discoveries
- **Academic Institutions**: Scholarly organizations that verify and archive
- **Guild Archives**: Faction-specific knowledge pools
- **Oral Traditions**: NPC bards and storytellers who spread information

## 5. Knowledge Valuation

Knowledge value is dynamic and contextual:

### 5.1 Value Factors
- **Exclusivity**: How many players possess this information?
- **Utility**: What practical advantages does it provide?
- **Verifiability**: Is the information confirmed or speculative?
- **Timeliness**: Does the information expire or become obsolete?
- **Danger Level**: How risky is it to acquire or use this knowledge?
- **Completeness**: Is it partial information or the full picture?

### 5.2 Market Dynamics
- **First Discovery Premium**: First person to document something holds monopoly
- **Depreciation**: Value decreases as information becomes common knowledge
- **Scarcity Creation**: Players may hoard or destroy knowledge to maintain value
- **Verification Services**: Scholars charge to confirm authenticity
- **Information Brokerage**: Players specialize in buying/selling intelligence

### 5.3 Non-Monetary Value
Knowledge provides advantages beyond gold:
- **Access**: Entry to restricted areas or societies
- **Safety**: Avoiding deadly threats through foreknowledge
- **Efficiency**: Optimizing routes, resource gathering, combat
- **Prestige**: Reputation as an expert or discoverer
- **Power**: Leveraging information for political influence

---

## 6. Knowledge Trading Systems

### 6.1 Player-to-Player Trade
Direct exchange mechanisms:
- **Barter**: Knowledge for knowledge, knowledge for items
- **Sale**: Fixed price or auction-based sales
- **Leasing**: Temporary access to information (e.g., map rental)
- **Partnerships**: Joint ventures sharing profits from discoveries
- **Bounties**: Payment for specific information requests

### 6.2 NPC Markets
Established venues for knowledge exchange:
- **Scholars**: Buy rare texts, sell translations and analysis
- **Cartographers**: Purchase exploration data, sell maps
- **Librarians**: Archive donations, provide research services
- **Informants**: Sell rumors and insider information (morally ambiguous)
- **Collectors**: Seek specific knowledge for personal collections

### 6.3 Information Black Markets
Illegal or restricted knowledge trade:
- **Forbidden Lore**: Necromancy, blood magic, dangerous rituals
- **Stolen Secrets**: Proprietary guild techniques, classified intelligence
- **Counterfeit Maps**: Deliberately false information sold as genuine
- **Assassination Contracts**: Target location and routine information

---

## 7. Knowledge Decay & Loss

Information is not permanently static:

### 7.1 Natural Decay
- **Forgotten Languages**: Without practitioners, translations become lost
- **Destroyed Records**: Fires, wars, disasters eliminate physical copies
- **Death of Experts**: Unique knowledge dies with its holder
- **Ecosystem Changes**: Species extinction eliminates biological knowledge

### 7.2 Intentional Suppression
- **Censorship**: Factions actively destroy or hide inconvenient truths
- **Secrecy**: Guilds protect proprietary techniques
- **Misinformation Campaigns**: Deliberate spreading of false data
- **Memory Alteration**: Magical or political rewriting of history

### 7.3 Rediscovery Mechanics
Lost knowledge can resurface through:
- Archaeological excavation of ruins
- Translation of newly found texts
- Oral traditions preserved by isolated communities
- Legacy Scenarios triggered by specific conditions
- Ancient Entities revealing forgotten truths

## 8. Integration with Other Systems

### 8.1 Legacy Scenarios
Knowledge acts as both trigger and reward:
- Knowing true names summons Ancient Entities
- Understanding rituals unlocks hidden powers
- Historical knowledge reveals secret paths
- **Reward**: Unique knowledge unavailable elsewhere

### 8.2 Role System
Roles determine knowledge acquisition rates:
- Scholars learn languages faster
- Hunters identify creature weaknesses automatically
- Wizards sense magical properties instinctively
- **Asymmetry**: Different roles create complementary knowledge pools

### 8.3 NPC Relationships
Knowledge affects social standing:
- Sharing discoveries builds trust and reputation
- Hoarding information creates suspicion or resentment
- False information damages credibility permanently
- **Memory**: NPCs remember what you told them and whether it was true

### 8.4 Economy
Knowledge drives market behavior:
- Resource location info affects supply/demand
- Crafting recipes create production monopolies
- Route knowledge influences trade profitability
- **Inflation**: Common knowledge reduces profit margins

### 8.5 Combat & Survival
Knowledge provides tactical advantages:
- Weakness exploitation increases damage efficiency
- Pattern recognition improves dodge/parry timing
- Environmental knowledge enables trap avoidance
- **Preparation**: Knowing what to expect reduces casualties

---

## 9. Anti-Exploit Measures

Preventing knowledge economy abuse:

### 9.1 Verification Systems
- **Peer Review**: Multiple players must confirm discoveries
- **Scholar Validation**: NPC experts authenticate major findings
- **Time Delays**: New information takes time to gain credibility
- **Source Tracking**: Origin of information is always recorded

### 9.2 Balance Mechanisms
- **Diminishing Returns**: Repeated similar discoveries yield less value
- **Difficulty Scaling**: Valuable knowledge requires proportional risk/effort
- **Randomization**: Some elements vary per world/server to prevent guides
- **Obfuscation**: Critical details may be encoded or metaphorical

### 9.3 Community Health
- **Reputation System**: Known liars lose trading privileges
- **Collaborative Incentives**: Group discoveries benefit all participants
- **Public Archives**: Essential survival info eventually becomes free
- **Whistleblowing**: Exposing fraud rewards the exposer

## 11. Success Metrics

Measure whether the knowledge economy is working:

### Quantitative Metrics
- Average knowledge trades per player per session
- Percentage of income derived from knowledge vs. traditional loot
- Number of unique discoveries documented per week
- Knowledge item circulation rate (how often traded)
- Ratio of verified vs. unverified information in circulation

### Qualitative Metrics
- Player discussions focused on discoveries ("Did you know...?")
- Emergent professions (information brokers, cartographers, scholars)
- Community collaboration on solving mysteries
- Stories shared about knowledge-based achievements
- "Wonder moments" triggered by new information

### Warning Signs
- Knowledge hoarding preventing progression
- Toxic misinformation campaigns
- Pay-to-win dynamics in knowledge access
- Guide dependency reducing discovery joy
- Knowledge becoming irrelevant compared to gear

## 12. Design Examples

### Example 1: The Hidden Valley
**Scenario**: A player discovers an unmarked valley with rare herbs.

**Knowledge Chain**:
1. Player explores off-map, finds valley (Discovery)
2. Herbalist role identifies unique plant properties (Analysis)
3. Player documents location in journal (Recording)
4. Player sells map to alchemists (Trade)
5. Map value decreases as more players visit (Depreciation)
6. Overharvesting depletes plants, location loses value (Consequence)
7. Player who discovered it gains "First Explorer" title (Legacy)

### Example 2: The Dragon's Weakness
**Scenario**: Players attempt to defeat an Ancient Entity.

**Knowledge Chain**:
1. Initial attempts fail, players observe attack patterns (Data Collection)
2. Scholar translates ancient text mentioning "heart of ice" (Research)
3. Wizard senses magical affinity during combat (Role Perception)
4. Group combines findings: weak to fire when tail is raised (Synthesis)
5. Strategy shared selectively with guild members (Controlled Distribution)
6. Guild defeats dragon, keeps method secret for future kills (Monopoly)
7. Rival guild pays heavily for the information (Market Value)
8. Eventually published in library, becomes common knowledge (Democratization)

### Example 3: The False Map
**Scenario**: A player creates and sells a fake treasure map.

**Knowledge Chain**:
1. Player draws convincing but false map (Creation)
2. Sells map to unsuspecting buyers (Fraud)
3. Buyers waste resources following false leads (Consequence)
4. Victims report scam to town guards (Reporting)
5. Seller's reputation tagged as "Untrustworthy" (System Response)
6. NPC merchants refuse to trade with seller (Social Penalty)
7. Seller must complete redemption questline or leave region (Resolution)

## 13. Technical Considerations

### Data Structure Requirements
- **Journal Database**: Per-player storage of all knowledge entries
- **Knowledge Graph**: Relationships between facts, sources, and verifications
- **Version Control**: Track changes to shared knowledge over time
- **Propagation Queue**: Manage rumor spread across server population
- **Valuation Engine**: Dynamic pricing based on scarcity and demand

### Performance Constraints
- Limit active knowledge items per player to prevent inventory bloat
- Batch process knowledge depreciation calculations
- Cache frequently accessed public knowledge
- Compress journal data for long-term storage
- Implement lazy loading for detailed knowledge entries

### Security Measures
- Server-side validation of all knowledge claims
- Anti-duplication checks for unique discoveries
- Audit trails for knowledge transactions
- Rate limiting on mass information distribution
- Encryption of sensitive player journal data

## 15. Conclusion

The Knowledge Economy System transforms information from a background element into a core gameplay pillar. By making knowledge valuable, tradable, perishable, and impactful, we create a living intellectual landscape where players become explorers, scholars, merchants, and guardians of truth.

This system directly supports our core pillars:
- **Wonder**: Unknown information drives curiosity
- **Discovery**: Finding knowledge feels meaningful
- **Community**: Sharing and debating information builds relationships
- **Living World**: Knowledge evolves and spreads organically
- **Legacy**: Discoverers are remembered for their contributions

When executed correctly, players will speak not of the sword they wielded, but of the secret they uncovered—and that is the ultimate success of this design.
